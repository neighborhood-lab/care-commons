## What This Rule Is (And Isn't)

Every year, CMS updates the Home Health Prospective Payment System (HH PPS). This proposed rule covers:

**What's included:**
- Payment rate updates for CY 2026
- Updates to the Patient-Driven Groupings Model (PDGM)
- Quality reporting requirements
- OASIS assessment updates
- Various technical adjustments

**What's NOT included:**
- EVV mandate changes (that's a Medicaid/21st Century Cures Act item, not Medicare)
- Conditions of Participation changes (separate rulemaking)
- Medicare Advantage home health policies (different CMS center)

Understanding scope prevents panic. If you're worried about EVV, this isn't the rule for you. If you're worried about Medicare FFS reimbursement, read on.

## The Payment Rate Impact

Let's cut to the number everyone cares about: **what happens to your payments?**

### The Headline Number

CMS typically proposes a market basket update (how much healthcare costs increased) minus a productivity adjustment (how much CMS expects providers to improve efficiency).

**Recent pattern:**
- Market basket increase: ~3-4%
- Productivity adjustment: ~0.3-0.4%
- Net update: ~2.5-3.5%

But here's where it gets complicated: CMS also applies "behavioral adjustments" based on how agencies responded to previous payment changes. These adjustments can significantly reduce (or occasionally increase) the net payment update.

### What This Means in Dollars

For a typical 30-day episode:

| Service Mix | Current Payment | Proposed Change | Impact |
|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|--------|
| Low-utilization | ~$1,800 | TBD% | +/- $XX |
| Average | ~$2,400 | TBD% | +/- $XX |
| High-acuity | ~$3,200 | TBD% | +/- $XX |

*These numbers will be updated when the specific rule is published.*

### The Real Impact: Case-Mix Weights

Payment rate changes matter, but case-mix weight recalibrations matter more. CMS periodically recalculates the weights for each of the 432 PDGM payment groups.

If your agency serves a patient population concentrated in groups that get reweighted down, your effective payment cut could be much larger than the headline number suggests.

**Action item:** Compare your case-mix distribution to the proposed weight changes. Run the numbers before the comment period ends.

## OASIS Updates

CMS periodically updates the OASIS assessment instrument. Common changes include:

**Data collection timing:**
- Start of Care (SOC) assessment requirements
- Recertification assessment timing
- Discharge assessment requirements

**Item modifications:**
- Functional status items (GG items)
- Clinical status items
- Risk assessment items

**Skip patterns and logic:**
- When certain items must be completed
- Conditional item requirements

### What This Means for Field Staff

Every OASIS change translates to:
- Updated training for clinicians
- Software configuration changes
- Quality measure recalculations
- Star rating implications

Plan for training time. Budget for it. Your clinicians will need to learn the new items before the effective date.

## Quality Reporting: What's New

The Home Health Quality Reporting Program (HH QRP) drives your star ratings on Care Compare. Changes to this program directly affect your public quality scores.

### Measure Changes

CMS regularly proposes:
- **New measures:** Adding metrics to the program
- **Removed measures:** Retiring outdated or duplicative measures
- **Modified measures:** Changing calculation methodology

### Why This Matters for Marketing

Your Care Compare star rating is visible to:
- Discharge planners at hospitals
- Case managers at SNFs
- Families researching agencies
- MCOs making network decisions

A half-star drop can cost you referrals. A half-star improvement can open doors.

**Action item:** Review proposed measure changes. Calculate your agency's projected performance under the new methodology.

## The Comment Period: Your Voice Matters

CMS is legally required to consider public comments on proposed rules. Most agencies don't comment. They should.

### Why Comment?

1. **CMS does read them.** Every comment gets reviewed. Thoughtful comments with data influence final rules.

2. **Industry associations amplify.** Your individual comment adds to the collective voice from organizations like NAHC, VNAA, and state associations.

3. **Documentation matters.** If a rule causes problems later, having commented creates a record of foresight.

### How to Write an Effective Comment

**Do:**
- Be specific. Reference exact sections of the proposed rule.
- Provide data. "This will cost my agency $X" is more compelling than "this is unfair."
- Offer alternatives. If you oppose something, suggest what would work better.
- Be professional. Emotion doesn't move regulators; evidence does.

**Don't:**
- Submit form letters. CMS counts these but weighs them less.
- Attack CMS or the administration. Focus on the policy, not the people.
- Wait until the last day. Comments submitted early get more attention.

### Where to Submit

Comments are submitted through the Federal eRulemaking Portal at **regulations.gov**. Search for the specific rule by CMS identifier (e.g., CMS-1766-P for the HH PPS rule).

**Comment deadline:** Typically 60 days from publication. Mark your calendar.

## Timeline: What Happens Next

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| Summer (typically July) | Proposed rule published |
| 60 days later | Comment period closes |
| Fall (typically November) | Final rule published |
| January 1 | Rule takes effect |

### The Gap Between Proposed and Final

CMS can (and does) change things between the proposed and final rule. Sometimes significantly.

**Examples of past changes:**
- Payment adjustment phase-ins extended
- Quality measure implementations delayed
- OASIS item effective dates pushed back

This is why commenting matters. The proposed rule isn't the final word.
