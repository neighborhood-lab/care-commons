# The $50,000 Question

Last month, a home health agency owner showed me her enterprise software renewal quote. $50,000 per year. For software that hasn't meaningfully improved since 2019.

"I asked them what's new," she told me. "They said they added a dashboard."

A dashboard. For $50,000.

This is the dirty secret of healthcare technology: most of your software budget doesn't fund engineering. It funds sales teams, account managers, implementation consultants, and shareholder returns. The actual software? That stopped improving years ago.

So where does the money go? And is there a better way?

---

Of that $50,000 renewal, maybe $7,500 goes to actual product development. The rest funds the machinery that convinces you to keep paying.

And those engineering dollars? They're split across hundreds of customers with different needs. Your feature request joins a backlog that will never clear. The "roadmap" they show you? Marketing fiction.

Meanwhile, venture-backed healthcare startups burn through capital building features no one asked for, chasing metrics that impress investors rather than serving users. When they run out of runway, your data is trapped in a system that stops getting updates.

This is the economics of proprietary healthcare software. It's not designed to serve you. It's designed to extract from you.

## The Enterprise Sales Machine

I used to work adjacent to enterprise sales. Here's how the machine works:

**Year 1**: Sales rep sells you a dream. Implementation takes 9 months. You finally go live in Q4.

**Year 2**: You discover the software doesn't quite work how the demo suggested. You submit feature requests. Nothing happens. You hire a consultant to customize workflows.

**Year 3**: Renewal comes up. Price increases 8-12%. You're too invested to switch. They know this. You sign.

**Year 4**: Same as Year 3. And Year 5. And Year 6.

The sales team that sold you the dream? They've moved on. The engineers who built the original system? They left for startups. You're paying for a brand, a contract, and a support ticket queue.

This is the enterprise model. Customer acquisition cost paid for through multi-year retention. Switching costs as a moat. Incremental price increases as a growth strategy.

It works beautifully—for shareholders. For home health agencies? It's extractive.

## What Community Ownership Changes

Community-owned software flips this equation. Instead of building value for shareholders, we build value for the community that uses the software.

```
Community-Owned Software Model
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Community Contributions   30-40%  ████████████████░░░░
Patreon/Sponsors          25-35%  ██████████████░░░░░░
Grants (Foundation/Govt)  20-30%  ████████████░░░░░░░░
Consulting (optional)     10-15%  ██████░░░░░░░░░░░░░░
Sales/Marketing           0-5%    ██░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░
```

No sales team. No account managers. No shareholders demanding 20% margins. Every dollar funds either direct development or community support.

The software improves because the community needs it to improve. Compliance rules change? Someone in the community notices and updates the code. State regulations differ? Agencies in each state contribute their knowledge.

This is the "rising tide lifts all boats" dynamic that proprietary vendors can never achieve. Their business model requires keeping knowledge proprietary. Ours requires sharing it.

## The Compounding Value of Shared Knowledge

Consider what we built with [Compliance Autopilot](https://care-commons.vercel.app/compliance):

- Texas HHSC regulations (26 TAC §558)
- Florida AHCA requirements (Chapter 59A-8)
- Employee Misconduct Registry requirements
- Level 2 background screening rules
- EVV aggregator integration specifications
- State-specific training hour requirements

One agency could never afford to research, implement, and maintain all of this. But when 50 agencies share the cost? When 100 agencies contribute their regulatory knowledge?

The math changes completely:

```
Enterprise Model (single agency):
  - Regulatory research: $10,000/year
  - Custom development: $25,000/year
  - Ongoing maintenance: $15,000/year
  Total: $50,000/year

Community Model (100 agencies):
  - Shared research: $100/year per agency
  - Shared development: $250/year per agency
  - Shared maintenance: $150/year per agency
  Total: $500/year per agency
```

Same functionality. 1% of the cost. And it actually gets better over time, because every contribution benefits everyone.

## Why Healthcare Specifically

You might wonder: why does healthcare need community-owned software more than other industries?

Three reasons:

### 1. Regulatory Complexity is Shared

Every home health agency in Texas faces the same 26 TAC §558 requirements. The regulatory knowledge is identical. It should be implemented once and shared, not reinvented by every vendor and purchased by every agency.

### 2. The Mission Alignment Problem

Enterprise software vendors optimize for revenue. Healthcare providers optimize for patient outcomes. These objectives conflict more often than they align.

When a vendor builds a feature, they ask: "Will this increase sales?" When a community builds a feature, they ask: "Will this help caregivers deliver better care?" Different questions lead to different software.

### 3. Data Portability Matters

When an enterprise vendor sunsets a product or raises prices beyond your budget, your data is hostage. With open source, your data is always yours. You can migrate, fork, or continue independently.

This is especially critical in healthcare, where continuity of care depends on continuity of data.
